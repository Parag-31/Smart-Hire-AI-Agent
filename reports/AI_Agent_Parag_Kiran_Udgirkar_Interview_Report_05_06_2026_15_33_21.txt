The four pillars of Object-Oriented Programming (OOP) are Encapsulation, Abstraction, Inheritance, and Polymorphism. Encapsulation is the process of combining data and methods into a single unit called a class and restricting direct access to some components, which helps in data protection and security. Abstraction means hiding the internal implementation details and showing only the essential features to the user, reducing complexity and making the system easier to use. Inheritance allows one class to acquire the properties and behaviors of another class, promoting code reusability and establishing a relationship between parent and child classes. Polymorphism means “many forms,” where the same method or function can behave differently depending on the object or context, improving flexibility and extensibility of the program.
